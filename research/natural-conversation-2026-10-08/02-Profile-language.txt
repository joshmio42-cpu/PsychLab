PsychLab: ordinary speech and authored small-talk facts
8 October 2026

Purpose

The eight fictional profiles need enough ordinary personal detail to sustain
brief casual follow-ups. Fluent wording alone cannot supply facts that the
profile never recorded. These changes provide a small, stable base for grounded
conversation without inventing a new biography during each answer.

Rewritten existing material

The existing goals, hobbies, communication preferences, relationships and
openings were rewritten in ordinary spoken language. Their meanings, priority
weights, life stages and caregiver roles were retained. For example, Jordan's
"I like a little space to reflect before answering. Concrete examples help me
explain what I mean" became "Give me a second to think. It’s easier if I can
give you an example."

Style instructions now favor showing those preferences through delivery instead
of repeatedly explaining them. They discourage forced slang, obligatory
hesitation, therapist-style reassurance and age or diagnosis stereotypes.
Concrete examples must come from the recorded case rather than being invented
to make the response sound convincing.

New authored fiction

Each profile now has two smallTalk entries, exported as personal facts P05 and
P06. These are newly invented details for fictional patients. They are NOT
observations from real people, case-study findings or diagnostic associations.
They do not come from the public sources used to study conversation behavior.
The details stay the same across seeds, diagnoses and interview difficulties.

Mika
P05: Usual choices include little block towers and animal picture books.
P06: The caregiver follows Mika's lead and looks through a book together when
     Mika brings one over; the caregiver's reason is shared activity.
These describe observed choices and the caregiver's own purpose, not Mika's
unspoken thoughts.

Leo
P05: Usual drawing subjects are houses; toy fruit is used in the pretend shop.
P06: The caregiver uses the shop game to offer choices about what to display
     and what the caregiver should pretend to buy.
These preserve a caregiver account and do not assign an internal motive to Leo.

Sam
P05: Usual comic subjects are robots and animals wearing silly hats.
P06: Making up stories grew from wanting the picture to continue into a story
     whose next event Sam could choose.

Riley
P05: Usual music includes pop and indie, with quiet songs and faster songs
     separated into playlists.
P06: Playlist-making began with interest in how track order changes the feel
     of the same songs.

Jordan
P05: Usual sketch subjects include mugs, plant pots and objects on the table;
     the usual medium is pencil.
P06: Sketching started with nearby objects because they needed no special
     setup and provided available subjects.

Avery
P05: Usual book choices are mysteries and short stories.
P06: Interest in mysteries began with trying to work out the ending; short
     stories could be finished in a shorter sitting.

Robin
P05: Usual gardening includes herbs in pots and a few flowers.
P06: Gardening began with a pot of herbs; noticing new growth prompted the
     original interest in continuing.

Alex
P05: Usual music includes instrumental music and guitar-heavy albums, normally
     played in track order.
P06: Whole-album listening began with curiosity about how songs fit together.

Boundaries for using these facts

Usual hobbies and past reasons for taking them up do not establish current
enjoyment, frequency, physical ability, symptom relief or treatment effects.
Current pleasure and functioning must come from the selected clinical case.
No artist names, book titles, real-person identities, exact places, ages,
medications or health explanations were added. The renderer should disclose
the particular detail relevant to the question, not recite all six personal
facts at every opportunity. A detail still absent from the case remains
unknown. The additions do not change the condition-specific case timeline.

Implementation
