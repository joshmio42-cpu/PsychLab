PsychLab: richer authored patient backgrounds
Authoring date: 2026-10-08
Data version: authored-backgrounds-1.0.0

Purpose

Give casual questions and requests for examples concrete, consistent answers.
The eight existing people each retain P01-P06 and gain six records, P07-P12:
48 new records overall, 12 personal facts per person. Existing names, life
stages, interests, relationships, and core communication preferences remain.

These are original fictional details. They are not transcribed patient cases,
clinical findings, validated developmental norms, or statements supported by a
psychology source. Their provenance is recorded as authored-fiction and their
clinical source lists remain empty. Research on conversation informs the goal
of having relevant examples; it does not provide or validate these biographies.

Record structure and temporal meaning

P07: a usual everyday routine or activity setup.
P08: a concrete earlier experience, before the simulated diary window.
P09: a usual shared activity or way of seeking support in an existing relationship.
P10: a preference about choice, plans, responsibilities, or support.
P11: a usual practical approach, without a guarantee that it works now.
P12: an unfulfilled future wish, explicitly distinct from an event that happened.

Every fact has a stable ID, readable topic label, domain, statement, provenance,
and scope (usual, past, preference, or wish). New records also have retrieval
keywords. Past records have timeframe: before-simulation. P05-P06 preserve their
meaning: caregiver play approaches are usual actions; the other hobby-origin
accounts are past experiences.

Authored examples

offering two activities; showing how toys go into a basket; a hoped-for play visit.
All observations and intentions are spoken by the caregiver. No child thoughts
or motives are supplied.

Leo, preschool child: crayons and a pretend shop; an earlier house drawing where

observations and choices, not accounts of Leo's private experience.

Sam, school-age child: a drawing folder; a previous robot-with-an-oversized-hat
comic; taking turns with a friend on picture ideas; wanting help without having
the whole picture finished for them; drawing a comic in parts; a future animal

Riley, adolescent: sorting song suggestions; comparing different playlist
openings; sharing a guitar passage or chorus with a friend; choosing a role in
a school project; talking through school options with a trusted adult; an idea
for an alternating-choice playlist. No platform, artist, romance, or family
conflict is inferred from adolescence or from a disorder selection.

Jordan, young adult: pencil and sketchbook setup; earlier mug sketches from two

The reason for preferring clear plans is still unknown. The cousin has no name.

Avery, adult: using a bookmark; a past incorrect guess at a mystery ending;

Robin, older adult: labeled herb pots and gardening tools; an earlier mint pot;

Alex, young adult: study and work commitments on a shared list; earlier comparison

No album titles, artists, or exact favorite are supplied.

Constraints

These background records do not mutate with seed, diary length, difficulty,
or assigned disorder. A newly chosen diagnosis must not rewrite who the person
is. Simulation changes remain in separate diary/case facts.

A usual interest or activity does not establish current pleasure or ability.
A usual practical method does not prove that it succeeds during symptoms.
An earlier background example must not be dated inside the recent diary.
A wish must not be treated as a completed activity or an arranged appointment.
No medication, exact age number, measured personality score, diagnostic status,
medical negative, new named relative, or hidden cause was added.

Validation

Added live/tests/authored-backgrounds.test.mjs. The new suite checks stable IDs
and temporal scopes, all compatible disorder selections for all eight people,
different seeds and diary lengths, deep immutability and clone isolation,
stage/caregiver constraints, preserved intentional unknowns, a selected P08 fact
through the generation pipeline, and legacy profiles without expanded fields.

The focused run with this suite plus individuality-difficulty.test.mjs and
patients.test.mjs passed all 19 tests. Selected-fact renderer checks use a mock
provider to inspect facts, calls and prompt size; they are not live-language
quality evidence. Live generation and full regression results are recorded by
the parent implementation/evaluation work.
