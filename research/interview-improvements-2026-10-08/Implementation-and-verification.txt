PsychLab — five interview improvements
Implemented and checked 8 October 2026

1. Stronger factual checks
The writer still paraphrases frozen source facts into ordinary speech. Independent
checks now cover selected unsupported names, motives, emotional benefits, current
health claims from usual habits, task-count interpretations, and added chronology.
Short contextual repairs distinguish an unknown fact from an unclear question,
keep a single recent example on one day, and retain caregiver knowledge limits.
A closed set of non-factual acknowledgments can be checked locally, saving the
third model call. All substantive answers retain the separate support audit.
There is no automatic retry or model fallback. Rejected candidates are not shown
as patient speech, and their incurred provider usage still contributes to cost.

2. Memory over longer interviews
A compact ledger remembers valid source IDs across the whole interview. It recalls
relevant old topics and includes a short canonical preview of a recalled diary
event. The ordinary transcript window remains four exchanges, up to 2,400 bytes.
Previous generated prose is never evidence for the writer or auditor. Optional
context is packed to the existing 12,000-byte limit, with omissions in the trace;
the question, canonical selected facts and candidate are never shortened to fit.
This adds no summarization requests and never mutates the frozen case.

3. Richer authored patients
All eight profiles now have 12 personal facts each, including 48 new records.
Earlier examples, usual routines, relationships, preferences and wishes carry
explicit provenance and time scope. They are stable across seeds, recent-day
windows and compatible disorder choices. Children retain the proper perspective.
Unrecorded medicine histories, favorite-album titles and relative names remain
unknown rather than being invented merely to fill out a profile.

4. A more useful finished-interview debrief
The debrief highlights exact learner questions and patient replies that referenced
new source material, unresolved areas and practical follow-up prompts. It flags
missing or mismatched checklist evidence links and absent turn references in the
learner's supporting explanation. A source match is only a lead, not proof that
every part of a checkpoint was established. Free-text conclusions are not graded
for diagnostic correctness. Feedback stays hidden until finish and persists in
History, refreshed sessions and conversation/debrief downloads. No AI calls added.

5. Estimated session cost
The sidebar shows USD estimates based on reported input/output tokens and versioned
public model rates. It distinguishes zero calls, estimated, partial and unavailable
costs. Provider metadata survives paid malformed/truncated replies, and completed
turn replay adds no charge. Old saved conversations are not assigned a fake zero.
History retains the session's rate snapshot; exports include its estimate and
source. Existing call caps remain 60 per interview and 300 per server run. These
are request limits, not account billing caps. Unreported cache savings are not
assumed. See ../natural-conversation-2026-10-08/09-Session-cost-estimates.txt.

Limits
The checks cover bounded classes of errors, not universal factual entailment.
Semantic auditing can miss an embellishment or reject a valid answer. A fact ID
does not prove every sentence, and a referenced paragraph can contain more than
the patient actually said. English topic/day retrieval is tested; equivalent
callbacks in other languages need further work. The evidence here evaluates a
fictional prototype and does not establish clinical validity or learner competence.
