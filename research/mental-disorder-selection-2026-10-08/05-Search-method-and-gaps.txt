SEARCH METHOD, COVERAGE AND OPEN GAPS
Date: 8 October 2026
Method: targeted narrative research for a proposed educational case selector.
This is not a systematic review or an exhaustive review of every DSM diagnosis.

Search sequence:
1. APA DSM-5-TR factsheets, publisher classification/organization, DSM-5 contents,
   update index and cumulative September 2025 supplement.
2. NIMH publications on depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, GAD, panic,
   social anxiety, OCD, PTSD, ADHD, autism, eating disorders, BPD and DMDD.
3. APA disorder-family pages: personality, dissociation, somatic symptoms, sleep,
   disruptive/conduct presentations, substance use, grief and related conditions.
4. CDC tic criteria, AACAP/NHS child anxiety and toileting resources, NIDCD
   developmental language material and APA learning-disorder resources.
5. WHO ICD-11 publication and gaming definition; VA DSM/ICD complex-PTSD distinction.
6. NICE delirium guidance, APA neurocognitive materials and MSD psychosis references.
7. Targeted checks of age, duration, nomenclature and conflicting public summaries.

Representative queries:
"DSM-5-TR fact sheets disorders updates 2025 2026"
"DSM-5-TR table of contents classification"
"ICD-11 clinical descriptions diagnostic requirements mental behavioural neurodevelopmental"
"DMDD age 6 18 12 months"
"Tourette diagnosis motor vocal tics one year before 18"
"complex PTSD ICD-11 DSM-5 differences"
"selective mutism separation anxiety children duration"
"brief psychotic disorder schizophreniform schizoaffective delusional disorder duration"
"major mild neurocognitive disorder independence delirium"
"enuresis encopresis diagnosis developmental age five four"

Selection:
Prefer APA for DSM naming and changes; authoritative national institutes and
professional bodies for public clinical descriptions; WHO for ICD classification.
Two MSD publisher summaries supply selected psychosis-duration cross-checks.
Search results also surfaced unofficial mirrors, forums and older materials;
these were not used to obtain or reproduce the full DSM manual.

Access levels:
The 56-item source register distinguishes opened pages/PDFs from search-result
excerpts. "Opened" means relevant returned text was reviewed, not that every
word of a long source was exhaustively read. Exact source dates are reported only
where observed; crawl dates are not publication dates. Undated is intentional.
No inaccessible page was counted as substantively read.

Access limitations:
- The full DSM-5-TR publisher book page returned HTTP 403.
- NINDS functional-neurological-disorder content was inaccessible.
- The NIA dementia page returned a bot-verification screen.
APA alternatives support terminology or overview statements; those access failures
are retained in the source register. No access controls were bypassed.
The WHO CDDR publication page was consulted; the entire 852-page manual was not read.

Version:
APA's checked update index listed September 2025 as its latest supplement. That is
the latest located on that page, not a guarantee that no later change exists anywhere.
DSM-5-TR was published in 2022; older DSM-5 sources in this pack remain labeled.
Do not treat public proposals as approved changes. Recheck APA before implementation.
No billing or ICD-10-CM codes are embedded in this research catalogue.

Specific findings requiring careful interpretation:
- DMDD: separate onset age from age at diagnosis. APA's 2022 child fact sheet [S47]
  supports 6–18; older APA condition fact sheet [S33] supplies onset context.
  NIMH's public age wording [S32] is narrower. Do not silently encode that summary.
- ARFID: the APA revision [S49] clarifies psychosocial interference can satisfy
  the relevant impact route. Low weight is not required in every ARFID presentation.
- BPD: adult-first cases are a project proposal, not a universal diagnostic ban on
  adolescents [S20]. Avoid absolute gates inferred from general public summaries.
- Neurodevelopment: a recent stressful event is not evidence that autism or ADHD
  was newly created. Developmental background and support context matter [S14–S15].
- Intellectual disability: an APA overview also discusses AAIDD terminology [S41].
  Do not substitute its organizational age definition for DSM's developmental rule.
- ICD comparison: CPTSD is not a separate DSM-5-TR diagnosis [S36]; gaming disorder
  in ICD-11 and DSM Internet Gaming Disorder's further-study status differ [S45–S46].
- Popular labels are not interchangeable: OCD/OCPD, panic attack/panic disorder,
  grief/prolonged grief, bipolar mood episodes/brief emotional shifts, and
  transgender identity/gender dysphoria need distinctions.
- Public descriptions sometimes simplify complex conditions or use pejorative
  framing. They are evidence references, not language templates for patient speech.

What this pack does NOT establish:
Full symptom-count algorithms; all specifiers; complete medical exclusions; age
gates for every option; comorbidity constraints for every pairing; disorder-specific
prevalence sampling; validated probabilities of symptoms; real treatment effects;
clinical accuracy of generated dialogue; or patient/learner diagnostic performance.
No diagnostic criteria checklists or assessment instruments are reproduced.

Coverage gaps explicitly marked in catalogue:
Brief psychotic disorder, delusional disorder, schizoaffective disorder and functional
neurological symptom disorder have naming/overview evidence but incomplete detailed
case-authoring evidence. Numerous other entries still require their exact duration,
developmental variants and exclusions to be verified. All 68 entries remain research
candidates, including the proposed first wave.

Broader DSM-family inventory not expanded into full candidate cards:
- Communication/motor/developmental: global developmental delay, speech sound,
  childhood-onset fluency, social (pragmatic) communication, developmental
  coordination, stereotypic movement and other tic categories [S01].
- Feeding/eating: pica, rumination and other specified presentations [S01].
- Sleep-wake: hypersomnolence, narcolepsy, breathing-related sleep conditions,
  circadian-rhythm disorders, parasomnias and restless legs [S24].
- Disruptive/impulse control: intermittent explosive disorder, kleptomania and
  pyromania [S01].
- Personality: paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal, antisocial, histrionic, narcissistic
  and dependent categories [S19]. The catalogue has BPD, avoidant and OCPD cards.
- Somatic-related: factitious presentations need dedicated evidence and portrayal
  review; do not infer deception from unexplained symptoms [S01; design inference].
- Sexual dysfunctions and paraphilic disorders are distinct DSM chapters [S55].
  They require dedicated specialist research and suitable adult clinical case
  design before individual options; this pack does not specify their criteria.
- Substance/medication-induced, other-medical-condition, other specified and
  unspecified categories require etiological/exclusion detail, not a generic label.
- Medication-induced movement effects and other conditions for clinical attention
  should not all be presented as standalone mental disorders [S55].
- Catatonia requires its own clinical-context research; do not automatically
  equate it with schizophrenia. Further-study conditions must retain their status.

Rights and reuse:
The pack uses short original paraphrases and links. It is not a substitute copy of
DSM-5-TR. Before embedding copyrighted criteria, instruments or licensed structured
interviews into a distributed product, review the applicable permission terms.
No unauthorized manual copy or full source archive is bundled.

Recommended next research after choosing the first cases:
For each chosen condition, verify the full applicable criteria and current updates;
then author multiple distinct fictional profiles, record exclusions and uncertainty,
obtain qualified case review, and evaluate the dialogue against those authored facts.
