AGE, HISTORY AND FUTURE DESIGN — RESEARCH RECOMMENDATIONS ONLY

1. THREE DIFFERENT KINDS OF TIME
Keep these separate:
- Life stage: the developmental context shown on the patient profile.
- Clinical background: onset, persistence, prior episodes, development, stressors,
  treatment and functional change before the recent simulation.
- Recent simulated days: a bounded sample of daily experiences leading up to the
  interview. The current app supports 1–90 days and then freezes the record.

Original design conclusion: adding more recent days must not manufacture the
earlier history needed to support a chosen condition. Diagnostic timing includes
symptom frequency, continuity, impairment and exclusions, not merely elapsed time.
A 12-month assessment window does not necessarily require symptoms to persist
continuously for all 12 months (for example substance-use assessment).

Selected source anchors (not full criteria):
- MDD: a major depressive episode uses a two-week anchor [S06].
- GAD and social anxiety: six-month history anchors [S09, S11].
- Panic disorder: recurrent unexpected attacks plus at least a month of related
  concern or change [S10].
- PTSD: longer than a month; acute stress disorder: three days to one month
  [S13, S29]. The trauma date and symptom-onset date are distinct.
- ADHD: six-month persistence, childhood onset and multiple settings [S14].
- Adult persistent depression requires a much longer history than 90 days [S06].
- Schizophrenia's overall course spans at least six months; schizophreniform
  disorder has a shorter bounded course [S43, S44].
- Chronic insomnia has a three-month/frequency anchor [S24, S25].
- Prolonged grief requires sufficient time since loss, with different adult and
  youth anchors [S17]. Cultural meaning remains relevant.

Do not convert calendar months into fixed 30-day blocks in a diagnostic validator.
Author calendar dates or explicit elapsed calendar periods, including events before
the recent log. No automatic diagnosis is justified by these few anchors.

Example design, not an actual case or new app behavior:
Riley / Adolescent / social anxiety / 28 simulated days.
The authored baseline could document a longer-established fear pattern, affected
settings and functional impact. The recent log then records selected school and
peer experiences. Choosing seven days would shorten the log, not erase onset.
Changing the seed could vary permitted recent events, but must not randomly erase
required history or add incompatible episodes.

2. LIFE STAGES AND INTERVIEW FORMAT
These are proposed starting scopes for fictional scenarios, not diagnostic rules.

Toddler:
Use caregiver observations and developmental information. Possible specialist
research directions include autism-related developmental assessment and ARFID
with appropriate feeding assessment [S15, S49]. Ordinary separation distress,
tantrums and toileting development must not become automatic disorders [S35, S51].
Do not simulate an adult-style autobiographical interview through the toddler.

Preschool child:
Use caregiver and preschool observations, with the child's communication where
appropriate. Developmental, feeding and selective-mutism scenarios need distinct
formats [S15, S34]. Silence should not be replaced by a fluent AI monologue.

School-age child:
School, home and peer context can support attention, learning, anxiety and other
carefully authored scenarios. Include caregiver/teacher collateral when required.
Language access and learning support must not be mistaken for disobedience [S14,
S39, S40]. Avoid assigning a psychiatric label to one classroom conflict.

Adolescent:
Include school, peers, growing autonomy and family context. Depression may be
expressed differently from adult stereotypes [S06]. Do not make every teenager
impulsive, traumatized or substance-using. Research age-sensitive conditions
individually before making them selectable.

Young adult and Adult:
Use varied work, study, relationship and independent-living contexts. The proposed
first authoring set starts here to reduce the number of developmental variants
needed at once. This is a scope choice, not an assertion that disorders occur only
in adults.

Older adult:
Preserve the person's autonomy and meaningful roles. Compare cognition and
functioning with their own earlier baseline. Depression, delirium and acquired
cognitive decline require different histories; older age alone is not dementia
[S28, S52, S53]. Caregiver collateral should supplement, not erase, the patient's
voice when they can participate.

Important age-boundary issue:
Life-stage labels overlap clinical cutoffs. For example, "Adolescent" alone does
not determine whether a specific youth rule applies. Preserve the user's preference
for phase labels, but require case-author verification of any applicable age gate.
Do not quietly generate a random birthday to satisfy a condition.

DMDD example:
APA's DSM-5-TR child fact sheet explicitly gives the diagnostic range 6–18; its
DMDD sheet also identifies onset before 10 [S47, S33]. The NIMH public summary
says diagnosis between 6 and 10 [S32]. Use the publisher's more precise distinction
for future authoring; do not treat onset and age at diagnosis as the same field.
Exact boundaries must be checked in the licensed manual before coding.

Personality-disorder example:
Adult-first case authoring is proposed. It is not a claim that diagnosis under
18 is categorically impossible: NIMH discusses selected younger BPD diagnoses
with a sustained severe pattern [S20]. Broad public wording [S19] is insufficient
for an absolute age block. Any youth variant needs its own reviewed evidence.

3. WHAT DISORDER SELECTION SHOULD MEAN LATER
A condition choice selects a reviewed fictional case formulation. It must not
equate a diagnosis with the patient's identity, all personality traits, motives,
dangerousness or every sentence they say.

Proposed future sequence, following the user's requested interaction:
Patient / life stage / recent days / seed
-> press Prepare frozen history
-> choose a condition family and a specific reviewed case
-> inspect a short case preview and interview format
-> confirm the selection
-> generate consistent recent events and freeze the final case
-> conduct the interview.

This flow is only documented here. The current button and routes are unchanged.
The actual freeze should occur AFTER the condition/case choice; otherwise the
existing history could conflict with a subsequently selected presentation.

Selection details worth deciding before implementation:
- A guided learning mode may reveal the chosen condition.
- A blind assessment needs an instructor-selected or concealed case label;
  choosing one's own visible diagnosis cannot be called fully blind.
- One primary condition initially; curated co-occurring conditions later.
  Arbitrary multi-select combinations are not necessarily clinically coherent.
- Include ordinary stress / no disorder assigned / uncertainty cases.
- Show all researched families, while distinguishing reviewed case availability
  from catalogue names awaiting clinical authoring. A name alone is not a case.
- A case can be referred for evaluation without pretending it has a confirmed
  diagnosis. Specify what the fictional patient knows versus reviewer information.

4. MINIMUM CASE EVIDENCE PACKAGE
Original design recommendation, not a DSM checklist:
a) Case ID, source edition, evidence IDs and revision date.
b) Life stage and explicitly verified developmental/age constraints.
c) Relevant baseline strengths, goals, relationships and communication needs.
d) Earlier history, onset and episode timeline.
e) Authored symptoms actually present, their frequency/context and functional impact.
f) Required diagnostic exclusions/differentials: confirmed, unknown or not assessed.
g) Medical and substance information when relevant; never invent negative findings.
h) Treatment and assessment history only when deliberately authored.
i) Present patient account, caregiver account and observed findings kept separate.
j) Known uncertain details and a disclosure plan.
k) Reviewer rationale, source-specific criteria verification and approval status.
l) Scenario-specific safety needs; no blanket assignment of suicidality or violence.
m) Recent events that fit the baseline, with an immutable case/version identity.

5. AGENT AND COST IMPLICATIONS FOR A LATER PHASE
Read-only code inspection: the current engine generates four broad everyday event
types, retains a frozen history and has no clinical diagnostic classifier. Numerical
tension/energy fields are experimental readouts. A selected disorder cannot be
implemented accurately merely by raising those values or changing the prompt to
"act depressed" or "act schizophrenic".

Suggested separation of responsibilities:
- Case authoring establishes longitudinal facts and reviewed constraints.
- Personality stays individually authored; diagnosis does not replace identity.
- Daily simulation varies only permitted situations/outcomes within that history.
- Memory retrieves selected facts and distinguishes perception from external events.
- Language renders the correct speaker and appropriate communication style.
- A support check rejects unsupported claims and contradictions.
- Review compares the transcript with authored evidence, without claiming that
  an LLM's approval is a clinical validation.

The catalogue and compatibility checks can run locally. Retrieve only the chosen
case's short approved facts; do not send this whole research pack to Featherless.
Keep current call/token budgets and avoid new per-day model requests. No price
estimate is asserted: provider billing and case prompt size still need measurement.
There were no Featherless calls during this research.

6. FUTURE VALIDATION
Before a case is labeled ready:
- Check the exact applicable DSM edition, revisions, criteria, exclusions and rights.
- Have an appropriately qualified reviewer assess the individual case and age variant.
- Check date arithmetic and consistency when switching days, seed, profile or condition.
- Keep episode boundaries and collateral accounts consistent across questions.
- Check caregiver voice, uncertainty and unknown medical history.
- Test misleading questions without allowing invented symptoms or past events.
- Review for stereotypes, cultural misinterpretation and false causal stories.
- Confirm text-only limitations for motor findings, mutism, cognition and medical exams.
- Verify no extra AI calls are triggered by browsing the selection screen.
- Ensure no diagnosis, scale score or competence rating is claimed merely because
  the model produced a fluent answer.
