Findings that matter for this project

Use DSM-5-TR as the versioned naming basis. The original DSM-5 contents are useful for the broad structure, but later additions and revisions must be retained. The checked APA update page lists September 2025 as its latest supplement; recheck before implementation. S01 · APA · S02 · APA · S03 · APA · S04 · APA

A short simulated history is not the entire clinical history. The current 1–90-day window cannot by itself establish a longstanding presentation. Our design recommendation is to author earlier clinical/developmental history separately, then generate recent experiences consistent with it. The catalogue records selected duration anchors and unverified requirements. S09 · NIMH · S14 · NIMH · S17 · APA

Age changes the evidence and speaker. A young child may require caregiver observations and developmental context. Adult symptoms should not simply be copied into toddler dialogue. Life-stage labels can remain in the interface while case authors explicitly verify applicable age boundaries. S15 · NIMH · S47 · APA

Some commonly requested options have different classification status. Complex PTSD belongs in a separately labeled ICD-11 comparison. Internet Gaming Disorder remains a DSM further-study condition, while WHO recognizes gaming disorder in ICD-11. Ordinary stress and diagnostic uncertainty deserve a separate training option rather than a forced disorder. S36 · VA National Center for PTSD · S45 · APA · S46 · WHO

The current agents are not disorder models. Read-only inspection shows a seeded everyday-event engine with experimental state readouts, not a diagnostic classifier. Adding a condition label or increasing simulated tension would not establish a credible case. The recommendation is a reviewed case-evidence layer, with existing agents expressing and retrieving only its authored facts.

Selection should precede the final freeze. For the later requested flow, Prepare frozen history would open a selection step; final history generation and freezing would follow the selected case. This is a design conclusion, not an implemented change.

Proposed authoring priorities

These tiers rank project preparation work, not the seriousness or worth of any patient. All entries remain unimplemented research candidates.

  • A — Proposed first authoring wave; review and case validation still required
  • B — Expanded authoring wave; additional course/specifier research required
  • C — Specialist case authoring or evidence/interface gaps; not a judgment about patients
  • D — Separate ICD-11 comparison, not a DSM-5-TR selectable diagnosis

Start by authoring eight focused condition options: major depressive disorder, GAD, panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, OCD, adjustment disorders, insomnia disorder and alcohol use disorder. Add a no-disorder/uncertainty comparator. For initial cases, adult profiles reduce the number of developmental variants to validate. This prioritization is an original design recommendation, not an endorsement from the sources.

The broader catalogue preserves child, developmental and more complex options for subsequent case authoring rather than presenting a diagnosis name as already simulation-ready.

Neurodevelopmental

Attention-deficit/hyperactivity disorder (ADHD)

B

DSM-5-TR

Persistent inattention and/or hyperactivity-impulsivity that interferes with functioning.

History to establish
At least six months; childhood symptoms before 12; evidence in multiple settings.
Suggested initial case stages
School-age child · Adolescent · Young adult · Adult · Older adult
Interview focus — authoring proposal
Daily organization and school/work examples; caregiver or school collateral.
Distinctions / limits
Sleep, anxiety, depression and learning difficulties can resemble attention problems. Toddlers are not a default ADHD case.

S35 · AACAP

Age, history and future integration

AGE, HISTORY AND FUTURE DESIGN — RESEARCH RECOMMENDATIONS ONLY

1. THREE DIFFERENT KINDS OF TIME Keep these separate: - Life stage: the developmental context shown on the patient profile. - Clinical background: onset, persistence, prior episodes, development, stressors, treatment and functional change before the recent simulation. - Recent simulated days: a bounded sample of daily experiences leading up to the interview. The current app supports 1–90 days and then freezes the record.

Original design conclusion: adding more recent days must not manufacture the earlier history needed to support a chosen condition. Diagnostic timing includes symptom frequency, continuity, impairment and exclusions, not merely elapsed time. A 12-month assessment window does not necessarily require symptoms to persist continuously for all 12 months (for example substance-use assessment).

Selected source anchors (not full criteria): - MDD: a major depressive episode uses a two-week anchor [S06]. - GAD and social anxiety: six-month history anchors [S09, S11]. - Panic disorder: recurrent unexpected attacks plus at least a month of related concern or change [S10]. - PTSD: longer than a month; acute stress disorder: three days to one month [S13, S29]. The trauma date and symptom-onset date are distinct. - ADHD: six-month persistence, childhood onset and multiple settings [S14]. - Adult persistent depression requires a much longer history than 90 days [S06]. - Schizophrenia's overall course spans at least six months; schizophreniform disorder has a shorter bounded course [S43, S44]. - Chronic insomnia has a three-month/frequency anchor [S24, S25]. - Prolonged grief requires sufficient time since loss, with different adult and youth anchors [S17]. Cultural meaning remains relevant.

Do not convert calendar months into fixed 30-day blocks in a diagnostic validator. Author calendar dates or explicit elapsed calendar periods, including events before the recent log. No automatic diagnosis is justified by these few anchors.

Example design, not an actual case or new app behavior: Riley / Adolescent / social anxiety / 28 simulated days. The authored baseline could document a longer-established fear pattern, affected settings and functional impact. The recent log then records selected school and peer experiences. Choosing seven days would shorten the log, not erase onset. Changing the seed could vary permitted recent events, but must not randomly erase required history or add incompatible episodes.

2. LIFE STAGES AND INTERVIEW FORMAT These are proposed starting scopes for fictional scenarios, not diagnostic rules.

Toddler: Use caregiver observations and developmental information. Possible specialist research directions include autism-related developmental assessment and ARFID with appropriate feeding assessment [S15, S49]. Ordinary separation distress, tantrums and toileting development must not become automatic disorders [S35, S51]. Do not simulate an adult-style autobiographical interview through the toddler.

Preschool child: Use caregiver and preschool observations, with the child's communication where appropriate. Developmental, feeding and selective-mutism scenarios need distinct formats [S15, S34]. Silence should not be replaced by a fluent AI monologue.

School-age child: School, home and peer context can support attention, learning, anxiety and other carefully authored scenarios. Include caregiver/teacher collateral when required. Language access and learning support must not be mistaken for disobedience [S14, S39, S40]. Avoid assigning a psychiatric label to one classroom conflict.

Adolescent: Include school, peers, growing autonomy and family context. Depression may be expressed differently from adult stereotypes [S06]. Do not make every teenager impulsive, traumatized or substance-using. Research age-sensitive conditions individually before making them selectable.

Young adult and Adult: Use varied work, study, relationship and independent-living contexts. The proposed first authoring set starts here to reduce the number of developmental variants needed at once. This is a scope choice, not an assertion that disorders occur only in adults.

Older adult: Preserve the person's autonomy and meaningful roles. Compare cognition and functioning with their own earlier baseline. Depression, delirium and acquired cognitive decline require different histories; older age alone is not dementia [S28, S52, S53]. Caregiver collateral should supplement, not erase, the patient's voice when they can participate.

Important age-boundary issue: Life-stage labels overlap clinical cutoffs. For example, "Adolescent" alone does not determine whether a specific youth rule applies. Preserve the user's preference for phase labels, but require case-author verification of any applicable age gate. Do not quietly generate a random birthday to satisfy a condition.

DMDD example: APA's DSM-5-TR child fact sheet explicitly gives the diagnostic range 6–18; its DMDD sheet also identifies onset before 10 [S47, S33]. The NIMH public summary says diagnosis between 6 and 10 [S32]. Use the publisher's more precise distinction for future authoring; do not treat onset and age at diagnosis as the same field. Exact boundaries must be checked in the licensed manual before coding.

Personality-disorder example: Adult-first case authoring is proposed. It is not a claim that diagnosis under 18 is categorically impossible: NIMH discusses selected younger BPD diagnoses with a sustained severe pattern [S20]. Broad public wording [S19] is insufficient for an absolute age block. Any youth variant needs its own reviewed evidence.

3. WHAT DISORDER SELECTION SHOULD MEAN LATER A condition choice selects a reviewed fictional case formulation. It must not equate a diagnosis with the patient's identity, all personality traits, motives, dangerousness or every sentence they say.

Proposed future sequence, following the user's requested interaction: Patient / life stage / recent days / seed -> press Prepare frozen history -> choose a condition family and a specific reviewed case -> inspect a short case preview and interview format -> confirm the selection -> generate consistent recent events and freeze the final case -> conduct the interview.

This flow is only documented here. The current button and routes are unchanged. The actual freeze should occur AFTER the condition/case choice; otherwise the existing history could conflict with a subsequently selected presentation.

Selection details worth deciding before implementation: - A guided learning mode may reveal the chosen condition. - A blind assessment needs an instructor-selected or concealed case label; choosing one's own visible diagnosis cannot be called fully blind. - One primary condition initially; curated co-occurring conditions later. Arbitrary multi-select combinations are not necessarily clinically coherent. - Include ordinary stress / no disorder assigned / uncertainty cases. - Show all researched families, while distinguishing reviewed case availability from catalogue names awaiting clinical authoring. A name alone is not a case. - A case can be referred for evaluation without pretending it has a confirmed diagnosis. Specify what the fictional patient knows versus reviewer information.

4. MINIMUM CASE EVIDENCE PACKAGE Original design recommendation, not a DSM checklist: a) Case ID, source edition, evidence IDs and revision date. b) Life stage and explicitly verified developmental/age constraints. c) Relevant baseline strengths, goals, relationships and communication needs. d) Earlier history, onset and episode timeline. e) Authored symptoms actually present, their frequency/context and functional impact. f) Required diagnostic exclusions/differentials: confirmed, unknown or not assessed. g) Medical and substance information when relevant; never invent negative findings. h) Treatment and assessment history only when deliberately authored. i) Present patient account, caregiver account and observed findings kept separate. j) Known uncertain details and a disclosure plan. k) Reviewer rationale, source-specific criteria verification and approval status. l) Scenario-specific safety needs; no blanket assignment of suicidality or violence. m) Recent events that fit the baseline, with an immutable case/version identity.

5. AGENT AND COST IMPLICATIONS FOR A LATER PHASE Read-only code inspection: the current engine generates four broad everyday event types, retains a frozen history and has no clinical diagnostic classifier. Numerical tension/energy fields are experimental readouts. A selected disorder cannot be implemented accurately merely by raising those values or changing the prompt to "act depressed" or "act schizophrenic".

Suggested separation of responsibilities: - Case authoring establishes longitudinal facts and reviewed constraints. - Personality stays individually authored; diagnosis does not replace identity. - Daily simulation varies only permitted situations/outcomes within that history. - Memory retrieves selected facts and distinguishes perception from external events. - Language renders the correct speaker and appropriate communication style. - A support check rejects unsupported claims and contradictions. - Review compares the transcript with authored evidence, without claiming that an LLM's approval is a clinical validation.

The catalogue and compatibility checks can run locally. Retrieve only the chosen case's short approved facts; do not send this whole research pack to Featherless. Keep current call/token budgets and avoid new per-day model requests. No price estimate is asserted: provider billing and case prompt size still need measurement. There were no Featherless calls during this research.

6. FUTURE VALIDATION Before a case is labeled ready: - Check the exact applicable DSM edition, revisions, criteria, exclusions and rights. - Have an appropriately qualified reviewer assess the individual case and age variant. - Check date arithmetic and consistency when switching days, seed, profile or condition. - Keep episode boundaries and collateral accounts consistent across questions. - Check caregiver voice, uncertainty and unknown medical history. - Test misleading questions without allowing invented symptoms or past events. - Review for stereotypes, cultural misinterpretation and false causal stories. - Confirm text-only limitations for motor findings, mutism, cognition and medical exams. - Verify no extra AI calls are triggered by browsing the selection screen. - Ensure no diagnosis, scale score or competence rating is claimed merely because the model produced a fluent answer.

Search method and evidence gaps

SEARCH METHOD, COVERAGE AND OPEN GAPS Date: 8 October 2026 Method: targeted narrative research for a proposed educational case selector. This is not a systematic review or an exhaustive review of every DSM diagnosis.

Search sequence: 1. APA DSM-5-TR factsheets, publisher classification/organization, DSM-5 contents, update index and cumulative September 2025 supplement. 2. NIMH publications on depression, bipolar disorder, schizophrenia, GAD, panic, social anxiety, OCD, PTSD, ADHD, autism, eating disorders, BPD and DMDD. 3. APA disorder-family pages: personality, dissociation, somatic symptoms, sleep, disruptive/conduct presentations, substance use, grief and related conditions. 4. CDC tic criteria, AACAP/NHS child anxiety and toileting resources, NIDCD developmental language material and APA learning-disorder resources. 5. WHO ICD-11 publication and gaming definition; VA DSM/ICD complex-PTSD distinction. 6. NICE delirium guidance, APA neurocognitive materials and MSD psychosis references. 7. Targeted checks of age, duration, nomenclature and conflicting public summaries.

Representative queries: "DSM-5-TR fact sheets disorders updates 2025 2026" "DSM-5-TR table of contents classification" "ICD-11 clinical descriptions diagnostic requirements mental behavioural neurodevelopmental" "DMDD age 6 18 12 months" "Tourette diagnosis motor vocal tics one year before 18" "complex PTSD ICD-11 DSM-5 differences" "selective mutism separation anxiety children duration" "brief psychotic disorder schizophreniform schizoaffective delusional disorder duration" "major mild neurocognitive disorder independence delirium" "enuresis encopresis diagnosis developmental age five four"

Selection: Prefer APA for DSM naming and changes; authoritative national institutes and professional bodies for public clinical descriptions; WHO for ICD classification. Two MSD publisher summaries supply selected psychosis-duration cross-checks. Search results also surfaced unofficial mirrors, forums and older materials; these were not used to obtain or reproduce the full DSM manual.

Access levels: The 56-item source register distinguishes opened pages/PDFs from search-result excerpts. "Opened" means relevant returned text was reviewed, not that every word of a long source was exhaustively read. Exact source dates are reported only where observed; crawl dates are not publication dates. Undated is intentional. No inaccessible page was counted as substantively read.

Access limitations: - The full DSM-5-TR publisher book page returned HTTP 403. - NINDS functional-neurological-disorder content was inaccessible. - The NIA dementia page returned a bot-verification screen. APA alternatives support terminology or overview statements; those access failures are retained in the source register. No access controls were bypassed. The WHO CDDR publication page was consulted; the entire 852-page manual was not read.

Version: APA's checked update index listed September 2025 as its latest supplement. That is the latest located on that page, not a guarantee that no later change exists anywhere. DSM-5-TR was published in 2022; older DSM-5 sources in this pack remain labeled. Do not treat public proposals as approved changes. Recheck APA before implementation. No billing or ICD-10-CM codes are embedded in this research catalogue.

Specific findings requiring careful interpretation: - DMDD: separate onset age from age at diagnosis. APA's 2022 child fact sheet [S47] supports 6–18; older APA condition fact sheet [S33] supplies onset context. NIMH's public age wording [S32] is narrower. Do not silently encode that summary. - ARFID: the APA revision [S49] clarifies psychosocial interference can satisfy the relevant impact route. Low weight is not required in every ARFID presentation. - BPD: adult-first cases are a project proposal, not a universal diagnostic ban on adolescents [S20]. Avoid absolute gates inferred from general public summaries. - Neurodevelopment: a recent stressful event is not evidence that autism or ADHD was newly created. Developmental background and support context matter [S14–S15]. - Intellectual disability: an APA overview also discusses AAIDD terminology [S41]. Do not substitute its organizational age definition for DSM's developmental rule. - ICD comparison: CPTSD is not a separate DSM-5-TR diagnosis [S36]; gaming disorder in ICD-11 and DSM Internet Gaming Disorder's further-study status differ [S45–S46]. - Popular labels are not interchangeable: OCD/OCPD, panic attack/panic disorder, grief/prolonged grief, bipolar mood episodes/brief emotional shifts, and transgender identity/gender dysphoria need distinctions. - Public descriptions sometimes simplify complex conditions or use pejorative framing. They are evidence references, not language templates for patient speech.

What this pack does NOT establish: Full symptom-count algorithms; all specifiers; complete medical exclusions; age gates for every option; comorbidity constraints for every pairing; disorder-specific prevalence sampling; validated probabilities of symptoms; real treatment effects; clinical accuracy of generated dialogue; or patient/learner diagnostic performance. No diagnostic criteria checklists or assessment instruments are reproduced.

Coverage gaps explicitly marked in catalogue: Brief psychotic disorder, delusional disorder, schizoaffective disorder and functional neurological symptom disorder have naming/overview evidence but incomplete detailed case-authoring evidence. Numerous other entries still require their exact duration, developmental variants and exclusions to be verified. All 68 entries remain research candidates, including the proposed first wave.

Broader DSM-family inventory not expanded into full candidate cards: - Communication/motor/developmental: global developmental delay, speech sound, childhood-onset fluency, social (pragmatic) communication, developmental coordination, stereotypic movement and other tic categories [S01]. - Feeding/eating: pica, rumination and other specified presentations [S01]. - Sleep-wake: hypersomnolence, narcolepsy, breathing-related sleep conditions, circadian-rhythm disorders, parasomnias and restless legs [S24]. - Disruptive/impulse control: intermittent explosive disorder, kleptomania and pyromania [S01]. - Personality: paranoid, schizoid, schizotypal, antisocial, histrionic, narcissistic and dependent categories [S19]. The catalogue has BPD, avoidant and OCPD cards. - Somatic-related: factitious presentations need dedicated evidence and portrayal review; do not infer deception from unexplained symptoms [S01; design inference]. - Sexual dysfunctions and paraphilic disorders are distinct DSM chapters [S55]. They require dedicated specialist research and suitable adult clinical case design before individual options; this pack does not specify their criteria. - Substance/medication-induced, other-medical-condition, other specified and unspecified categories require etiological/exclusion detail, not a generic label. - Medication-induced movement effects and other conditions for clinical attention should not all be presented as standalone mental disorders [S55]. - Catatonia requires its own clinical-context research; do not automatically equate it with schizophrenia. Further-study conditions must retain their status.

Rights and reuse: The pack uses short original paraphrases and links. It is not a substitute copy of DSM-5-TR. Before embedding copyrighted criteria, instruments or licensed structured interviews into a distributed product, review the applicable permission terms. No unauthorized manual copy or full source archive is bundled.

Recommended next research after choosing the first cases: For each chosen condition, verify the full applicable criteria and current updates; then author multiple distinct fictional profiles, record exclusions and uncertainty, obtain qualified case review, and evaluate the dialogue against those authored facts.
