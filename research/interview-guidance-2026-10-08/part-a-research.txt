PSYCHLAB INTERVIEW GUIDANCE — PART A
Research access date: 2026-10-08
Coverage: catalogue indices 0–22 inclusive: adhd, autism, learning, language, idd, tourette, mdd, pdd, dmdd, pmdd, bipolar1, bipolar2, cyclothymia, gad, panic, social_anxiety, specific_phobia, agoraphobia, separation, selective_mutism, ocd, bdd, hoarding.

PURPOSE AND METHOD
Read the current live/data/disorder-catalogue.json and existing source register, then independently searched and opened authoritative public sources. The accompanying JSON contains 23 disorder-specific educational guides, with 116 original inquiry checkpoints, course anchors, differential prompts, one relevant assessment approach/reference, and age/safety cautions. It includes child-only options as well as the conditions available to young adults.

These are original teaching questions, not copied DSM criteria, a validated checklist, an administered screening instrument, or a diagnostic scoring system. Checkpoint status records what the learner explored and what evidence was elicited. It must not turn a completed checklist into a diagnosis, nor infer symptom absence from an unanswered question. Learners should retain uncertainty, alternative explanations, functioning and temporal evidence.

SOURCE SCOPE AND LIMITS
Full licensed DSM-5-TR was not accessed. APA patient summaries, an original DSM-5 publisher fact sheet, federal health guidance, original assessment research and publisher/author-hosted instrument guidance support this teaching layer. Public summaries omit exclusions, specifiers and detailed rules. Timelines are inquiry anchors, not a complete implementation of diagnostic criteria. Sources are dated by their own publication where known; access today does not turn an older paper into current validation. The app's age-stage labels do not supply the exact chronological age needed to administer age-restricted scales.

ASSESSMENT INTERPRETATION
For developmental presentations, use appropriate specialist observation, developmental records and caregiver/teacher collateral. A fluent text exchange cannot establish motor tics, intellectual ability, autism, language performance or selective mutism. Infant/toddler cases especially need proxy/observational evidence and cannot be treated as adult self-reports.

Where there is a suitable instrument, the JSON links to an external source and describes its purpose; it does not reproduce the form or compute its score. APA depression (adapted PHQ-9) and several severity forms use a seven-day interval, which must not be substituted for the disorder's duration. The linked parent ARI is an age 6–17 adaptation; its window differs from the original six-month ARI, and it measures irritability rather than a DMDD diagnosis. APA's instrument pages specify reproduction rights, including limits on modification/electronic reuse. No permission has been assumed for embedding their items.

The BDDQ screens; an interview follows a positive result. BDD-YBOCS describes severity in diagnosed BDD and must not be substituted as a diagnostic screen. The HRS-I study describes an assessment of hoarding severity/impact, not a universal diagnostic shortcut. DRSP evidence reviewed was the 2006 original-study abstract; it concerned adult DSM-IV-era assessment. The current PMDD guide requires prospective cycle evidence but intentionally does not claim a complete DSM-5-TR diagnostic algorithm.

NOTABLE RECONCILIATIONS
* DMDD: NIMH's public brochure uses a simplified diagnosis-age range of 6–10. APA's publisher fact sheet distinguishes onset before 10 from first diagnosis ages 6–18; that more precise distinction is used. APA also explains why DMDD and ODD should not automatically both be assigned.
* PDD and cyclothymia need a long adult or youth history. Adjustable simulated days cannot manufacture the required developmental or longitudinal evidence.
* Mania versus hypomania requires a distinct episode, changed functioning and severity context. A current depression presentation alone cannot distinguish bipolar II.
* Tourette's does not require swearing. Functional impact is explored for needs, but absence of impairment alone does not exclude the diagnosis.
* Selective mutism: ASHA supports setting comparison, duration beyond initial school adjustment, language/hearing evaluation and an interdisciplinary approach. Nonverbal communication should be available rather than forced speech.
* Specific-phobia six-month timing was corroborated in SAMHSA's official indexed Table 3.11. Direct PDF access returned 403 and the NCBI mirror displayed a browser challenge; this is an extract-level timing reference, not a claim to have read the full report.
* Questions about suicidality, severe manic disruption, medical causes of panic, and household hazards describe assessment needs, not risk prediction from a disorder label. Screening scores never override an immediate concern.
* Differential lists are concise educational comparisons to investigate, not exhaustive lists or ruled-out diagnoses.

VALIDATION
Compared JSON IDs and names to current catalogue indices 0–22: all 23 match. Each entry has 4–6 unique checkpoint IDs (five per entry, six for MDD), 2–4 differentials, nonempty course/test/cautions and source links. No app files are modified by this research part.

FINAL CROSS-PART REVIEW, 8 OCTOBER 2026
PMDD now explicitly asks for prospective daily records across at least two menstrual cycles. The current NHS PMDD page was opened and the diagnosis/diary section inspected; it recommends a symptom diary for at least two menstrual cycles. This clarifies the observation process, not a rule that two diary cycles alone establish a diagnosis.
