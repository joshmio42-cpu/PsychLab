PURPOSE AND METHOD
This file supports an educational fictional interview simulator. The companion part-b.json contains original inquiry prompts, relevant time-course distinctions, plausible alternatives to explore, and references to genuine assessment approaches. The checkpoints are neither exhaustive diagnostic criteria nor a validated scale. Supported / Not supported / Unclear / Not asked labels document evidence and gaps; they must not be converted into a clinical diagnosis or a score threshold.

Existing project references were used as a starting map. Current public APA, AACAP, NIH, VA, NHS, NICE and original assessment-study sources were actively checked. Public summaries and open primary-study abstracts were used; the full copyrighted DSM-5-TR text was not obtained or audited. No DSM criteria, full copyrighted questionnaire, proprietary scoring rule or purported validated diagnostic algorithm is reproduced here. Sources marked "indexed excerpt" were inspected in the official source's search excerpt; their live pages were blocked or challenged. This is a limitation, not a claim of full-page access.

The questions are original educational prompts. Mentioning a named assessment does not mean the simulator administers that assessment. Real use requires the appropriate authorized version, population, language, administration and clinician interpretation. The reference instrument and its educational description must remain separate from the simulator's own checklist.

IMPORTANT EVIDENCE AND IMPLEMENTATION LIMITS
- PTSD symptoms persisting beyond one month must be distinguished from acute stress disorder's 3-day to 1-month period. PCL-5 supports adult symptom screening/monitoring and provisional assessment; it does not replace a clinician interview such as CAPS-5. Use a developmentally appropriate assessment for youth.
- The VA reports limited validation of updated DSM-5 acute-stress instruments. ASDI-5 is a trained-clinician assessment reference, not an automatic test.
- Adjustment timing is anchored to a stressor: onset within three months; continuation beyond six months after the stressor and its consequences cease calls for reconsideration.
- Prolonged grief timing differs by age: at least 12 months after the death for adults and six months for children/adolescents, with cultural context and impairment essential. Timing alone never establishes a disorder.
- Reactive attachment disorder concerns early childhood and is not interchangeable with attachment style. Disinhibited social engagement disorder is not simply friendliness. Neither can be inferred from a child's dialogue or presumed neglect. Specialist developmental assessment and verified caregiver/placement information are needed.
- Dissociative experiences require medical, neurological, substance, cultural and sleep-related alternatives to be considered. Do not suggest hidden trauma or treat generated recollections as recovered memories.
- Somatic symptom disorder does not require medically unexplained symptoms. Persistent symptomatic course is typically longer than six months. Illness anxiety generally involves at least six months of health preoccupation, although the feared condition can change.
- Functional neurological disorder requires clinically identified positive signs and an appropriate neurological assessment; a stressful event or normal scan alone does not establish it. Text chat cannot perform the relevant examination.
- Eating disorder assessment includes developmental growth and medical stability at all body sizes. Bulimia and binge-eating disorder frequency/course summaries use an average of at least weekly over three months. Medical assessment cannot be replaced by a screen, appearance or weight alone.
- ARFID may meet the functional-interference component without low body weight. PARDI's cited initial study involved ages 10–22; later or other versions must be selected and checked for the intended age. The cited pilot does not by itself validate use with toddlers.
- Bedwetting and soiling must be interpreted against toileting development and medical causes such as constipation, not punished. The educational entries deliberately do not invent exact DSM age/frequency algorithms from general public guidance.
- Chronic insomnia requires sufficient sleep opportunity, daytime consequences and a course of at least three nights a week for at least three months. The adult ISI's recent reporting window does not independently verify the chronic course; pediatric assessment requires age-appropriate methods.
- ODD's pattern is assessed over at least six months. Conduct assessment covers the previous 12 months with recent behaviour in the previous six months. Neither is established by ordinary age-related disagreement, a single incident, dangerousness assumptions or a checklist sum.
- The SDQ is broad age-appropriate screening that can gather parent, teacher and youth perspectives; it does not diagnose ODD or conduct disorder.
- The MGH Hairpulling Scale citation is adult development research. The Skin Picking Scale citation is the original 2001 SPS, not the revised SPS-R. No numerical cutoffs are imported.
- Medical urgency, immediate safety, developmental limitations and caregiver handling are specific to the relevant entries. Generated cases remain fictional and require expert review before education claims.
