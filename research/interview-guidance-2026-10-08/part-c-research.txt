What this research supports
These are original educational interview prompts organized around relevant history, functioning, context and competing explanations. They are not reproduced DSM criteria, validated test items, diagnostic thresholds or a complete clinical interview. Asking or ticking a prompt does not establish that a criterion is present. A trainer should link any conclusion to actual recorded evidence and mark missing information as unknown.

Public APA/NIH/CDC/WHO clinical summaries and published assessment guidance were searched and opened where available. No licensed complete DSM-5-TR manual or proprietary SCID item set was accessed. The DSM-5 table from SAMHSA is historical context. Public summaries omit details, specifiers and exceptions; the app must not claim full DSM coverage or clinical validation.

Assessment choices
AUDIT-C, TAPS, PGSI, SCID-5/SCID-5-PD, 4AT and ITQ are named as real assessment references, with links and interpretation limits. The proposed feature does not administer, reproduce, calculate or invent their scores. Cognitive cases recommend comprehensive assessment with examples of brief detection tools; gender dysphoria and ICD-11 gaming use an explicitly described clinical assessment rather than an invented diagnosis quiz. Assessment age, language, context, administration conditions and applicable licensing must be reviewed before any later instrument implementation.

Key integration distinctions
- Longer-term evidence must come from the authored premorbid/longitudinal history. Simulating extra days cannot create childhood onset, a past-year use-disorder window or a six-month course retrospectively.
- For psychosis: brief psychotic disorder requires recovery within its brief course; schizophreniform is the intermediate course; schizophrenia requires its longer overall disturbance and active-phase review. Schizoaffective requires a mood–psychosis timeline, including psychosis outside a major mood episode and the majority-course mood requirement. Mood-linked psychosis alone does not meet that distinction.
- Delirium is an urgent medical-assessment scenario; a quiet or fluctuating presentation must not be mistaken for a normal interview or simple dementia. 4AT guidance is setting-sensitive.
- Substance use, physiological dependence, intoxication, withdrawal and use disorder are different constructs. Appropriate supervised opioid tolerance/withdrawal does not itself establish OUD. Benzodiazepine interruption, severe alcohol withdrawal and opioid overdose require medical urgency rather than a quiz result.
- Personality cases require longstanding, cross-context evidence. An intense emotional episode or recent anxious week is insufficient; bipolar episodes and trauma-related conditions remain alternatives.
- Gender diversity is not a disorder. Assess distress and functioning, development, social adversity and the person's own goals without using identity or pronouns as diagnostic evidence.
- CPTSD and gaming entries retain their ICD-11 comparison status. CPTSD requires PTSD plus the additional self-organization pattern; repeated trauma by itself is insufficient. Gaming hours alone do not establish disorder.
- A named assessment is not interchangeable with the original five prompts. Educational checklist completeness must not be reported as a probability, validated score or confirmation of diagnosis.

Final cross-part review, 8 October 2026
Cannabis, stimulant, sedative and tobacco prompts now explicitly inquire about the past 12 months for consistent longitudinal history. The NIDA TAPS reference was reopened and confirms its initial screening window is the previous 12 months, with follow-up questions covering three months. This supports the inquiry window only; it is not a claim that screening duration establishes DSM criteria, nor a rule excluding a shorter emerging problem. No TAPS responses or scores are implemented.
