PSYCHLAB — DIFFICULTY AND INDIVIDUAL PATIENTS
2026-10-08

Choose Interview difficulty below the patient, days and seed controls. It stays
visible during the interview, survives refresh and is saved with the conversation
and debrief. Standard is the default. Existing saved conversations still open.

BEGINNER
A clear opening includes the concern and the person's own goal. Relevant current
experiences can be offered up front. Up to four relevant facts can be supplied for
an answer, with the model selecting the smallest useful set.

STANDARD
A natural opening includes the concern and personal goal. Up to two relevant
facts are supplied per answer. Follow-up questions bring out the rest.

CHALLENGING
The first broad opening uses an authored, less detailed introduction. One relevant
fact at a time is available for an answer. Focused questions still reach the same
recorded course, experiences, relationships and goals; they are never locked behind
a trust score or a prescribed question order. General follow-ups can develop an
opening into experiences, earlier course and impact. A repeated opening can become
more specific. Difficulty does not cause hostility, new symptoms, forgotten history
or false denials. Caregiver and supported-written perspectives take precedence.

Difficulty is a session delivery setting, separate from the frozen snapshot.
The same patient, selected condition, seed and days produce the same private
history hash at all three levels. Each public session still has an opaque reference.
Random may select a different condition when prepared again, as before.

EIGHT DISTINCT PEOPLE
Each profile now has authored personal goals, usual interests, communication
preferences and relationships. Jordan values sketching and quiet reflection;
Alex wants a manageable study/work plan and time for music; Avery values reading
and sharing responsibilities; Robin values gardening and community participation.
Sam and Riley have their own school, creative and relationship priorities. Mika's
and Leo's caregiver accounts describe different observed interests and support.
Each person also has two stable fictional small-talk facts: a concrete hobby
detail and its origin or motivation. For Mika and Leo these describe the caregiver's
observations and own reasons for joining an activity, never the child's thoughts.
These details are newly authored fiction, not facts borrowed from real case studies.
They are citable personal facts, but do not establish a diagnosis by themselves.
Usual interests and their origins do not establish present enjoyment, activity
frequency, physical ability, symptom relief or recovery. Ordinary openings include a
personal goal, so a shared condition does not make two patients' openings identical.

The personality agent receives each person's own authored goal priorities, which
affect its existing local action rules. These engineering weights are neither
measured personality scores nor age/diagnosis-based norms. The profile does not
change when the condition, seed, day count or difficulty changes. Numerical values
remain private. Caregivers do not claim access to a child's private thoughts.

CONVERSATION AND RESPONSE GROUNDING
The earlier exact-statement renderer sounded repetitive in casual conversation.
LANGUAGE now rewrites the relevant permitted facts in ordinary first-person speech,
usually one short sentence and no more than three. It may change wording, not
events, relationships, timing, frequency, certainty or speaker identity. Contractions
and short answers are encouraged; forced slang, random fillers, repeated declarations
of communication preferences and invented examples are not.

Routing distinguishes substantive questions, social gestures, unavailable details,
ambiguity and system boundaries. A greeting receives a greeting; an acknowledgment
does not trigger a complaint summary. Hobby follow-ups can reach the two recorded
casual facts. A new named topic overrides the previous one. Recent conversation
resolves references but is not evidence: the renderer and auditor must ground
factual claims in the selected case facts, not earlier generated prose.

Unavailable details use one routing call and a labeled system boundary: "That
detail isn’t available for this interview." This is not patient speech, a denial,
memory loss or refusal. Social and unavailable turns remain visible and can be
saved in conversation history, but cannot be selected as clinical checklist evidence.
Older saved histories without interaction metadata still open.

Deterministic checks enforce valid fact IDs and catch specified clinical inventions,
numbers, duration/frequency changes and internal wording. AUDITOR checks the actual
reply against the cited facts, including unsupported everyday details. Rejected
answers are not displayed and are not silently replaced with a scripted answer.
No automatic paid retry is made. Semantic checking remains fallible; this design
does not establish that every accepted reply is factually correct or clinically
valid. Frozen-case consistency and naturalness both need continuing evaluation.

USAGE
Preparation, individuality and disclosure policy run locally with no API calls.
A supported answer or generated social reply still uses up to three model calls.
An unavailable-detail or other system boundary uses one routing call. CORTEX has
128 output tokens, LANGUAGE 320 and AUDITOR 160 (608 combined maximum allowance).
The input cap remains 12000 UTF-8 bytes per call, with 60 attempted calls per
interview and 300 per server run. A more gradual interview can require more
questions and therefore cost more in total; no fixed bill estimate is implied.

VERIFICATION
The final automated suite passed 127 checks covering profiles, frozen-history
consistency, disclosure limits, case grounding, social/clinical separation,
missing-detail boundaries, archives and budgets. The final 72B-model live sample
completed 16 turns without request errors. This is an operational result, not a
claim that all prose was factually validated: minor unsupported flourishes passed
the audit, including calling sketch subjects "interesting shapes" and describing
an album as telling a story.
Focused transcripts, diagnostics and earlier failures are preserved separately
in ../../research/natural-conversation-2026-10-08/. They do not establish reliable
behavior across all profiles/cases, educational effectiveness or clinical validity.
