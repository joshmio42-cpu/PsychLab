PSYCHLAB — DISORDER SELECTION AND PREPARATION
2026-10-08

HOW TO USE
Choose a patient, recent simulated days and seed. Prepare frozen history now opens
a centered selection card. Use its arrows, arrow keys or mouse wheel to move
between the cases available for that life stage. Choose a named case, Random,
or No disorder, then prepare the interview.

The catalogue includes 65 DSM-5-TR research candidates and two separately labelled
ICD-11 comparisons (complex PTSD and gaming disorder). No disorder is a separate
ordinary-life option. Stage filtering reflects the authored scenarios; it is not
a statement that every omitted age/condition combination is clinically impossible.
Some broad life stages cannot verify exact diagnostic age gates. No numerical age
or diagnosis is invented to fill that gap.

WHAT PREPARATION DOES
The server prepares the profile, earlier course, current experiences, daily events,
personality context and remembered interpretations. These are frozen before the
interview. A longer recent window gives more recorded days, not a different age.
Earlier histories establish an authored longer course independently of the diary.
Short presentations occupy only their relevant recent interval; resolved brief
episodes remain in earlier-history facts. Premenstrual examples have fictional
cycle windows rather than symptoms on every day. The diary is not a medical model.

The loading screen reports actual server phases and completed days. Preparation
is local and uses zero Featherless calls. It can finish quickly. Cancel removes
the job; stale work cannot overwrite a newer preparation. Refresh reconnects to
the owned job, and network failures offer a status check before another creation.

HOW THE AGENTS USE IT
The condition is represented by unlabeled fictional experiences and functional
impacts, not by asking the model to impersonate a diagnosis. CORTEX considers
authored interpretations; MNEMOSYNE stores them alongside ordinary stage-specific
events. The personality projection keeps relationships, goals and uncertainty.
The response model receives selected facts and a compact expression context.
These narrative premises do not imply calibrated disease effects on numerical
tension, energy or personality traits. A label never defines a personality type.

Toddler and preschool interviews use a caregiver. Selective-mutism patient cases
use supported written responses. Developmental/communication cases keep their
individual voice and explicitly authored support, without invented test scores.

MYSTERY CASES
Random selects one compatible case on the server and keeps that choice for the
preparation and interview. Its label, description and sources are withheld from
public session/progress responses and from the model's evidence packet. Its
public frozen reference is opaque, not the private reproducible snapshot hash.
The case answer is revealed only after an answered interview and completed learner
reasoning. Saved conversation history contains disclosed speech, not hidden data.
The chosen label is withheld from model prompts; generated replies also undergo
the existing evidence audit and a direct selected-label check. Semantic model
auditing is fallible, so this is not a secure or validated examination system.

A seed reproduces the local diary for the same patient, case and number of days.
Random case selection is separate: preparing Random again may choose another case
even with the same seed. AI wording is not seed-reproducible.

COST AND LIMITS
Supported replies keep the existing three-call route/render/audit flow. Earlier
facts plus at most 12 locally retrieved daily topics keep long diaries compact;
explicit day questions receive priority. Existing prompt/output caps and session/
process call budgets still apply. No automatic paid retry or extra preparation
model call was added. Provider billing remains controlled by the user's account.

CONTENT AND PROVENANCE
The saved research is in ../../research/mental-disorder-selection-2026-10-08.
The private runtime catalogue is live/data/disorder-catalogue.json; original
fictional premises are in live/disorders.mjs. Reviewer output contains the case
source IDs and explicit limitations. The catalogue draws on public APA DSM-5-TR,
NIMH, WHO, CDC and other referenced summaries, not a reproduced DSM manual.

All 67 named options have distinct authored course, experiences, impact and diary
observations. They are teaching formulations, not complete criteria sets or
clinically approved cases. Missing medical/developmental/substance exclusions stay
unknown. Selecting a label is author intent, not proof of a diagnosis. No disorder
means no assigned formulation, not a declaration of lifelong mental health.

The original foundation documents and ZIP are historical snapshots.
