PATIENT LIFE STAGES — 2026-10-08

The patient selector contains eight fictional profiles. Age appears as a life
stage, with no generated numerical ages or birthdays:
Mika: Toddler (caregiver interview; 7 days initially selected)
Leo: Preschool child (caregiver interview; 14 days initially selected)
Sam: School-age child (schoolwork, play, caregiver support; 28 days)
Riley: Adolescent (school projects, peers, trusted-adult support; 28 days)
Jordan: Young adult (work, friends, cousin support; 28 days)
Avery: Adult (work and household tasks, friends, sibling support; 28 days)
Robin: Older adult (community activities, neighbors, friends, gardening; 28 days)
Alex: Young adult (the existing fixed authored case and assessment).

These are individual authored scenarios, not claims about everyone in that age
group. No diagnosis, cognitive impairment or measured personality is assigned
because of age. Alex retains the original authored source data; the profile
display uses a stage label.

WHAT DAYS CHANGE
Every generated profile supports 1–90 recent days. Each day adds one recorded
event, an action and consequence, memory, and experimental internal state updates.
The profile determines the event wording, available relationships, interpretations,
and activity outcomes. A school task replaces an employment task for a child.
The same profile and seed reproduce the same history; a longer run preserves its
earlier days. The resulting history is frozen before the interview.

Days describe a recent observation window while already in the selected stage.
They are not elapsed time since birth. Increasing days does not advance a child
to another stage, simulate developmental milestones, or author earlier medical
history. The 7/14/28 defaults are interface choices, not scientifically prescribed
observation periods. All stages share the same 90-day software limit.

HOW THE AGENTS USE THE STAGE
The local engine uses the selected profile and relevant support relationships.
The AI receives the life stage, speaker, selected memories and bounded expression
guidance. Toddler and preschool interviews use a caregiver's observed account;
private child thoughts remain unknown. School-age speech uses concrete language.
The auditor is also told which person is speaking. Saved conversations preserve
the life-stage label and caregiver speaker. Preparing any history uses no API
calls; the existing three-call checked-answer flow and budgets are unchanged.

LIMITS OF THE IMPLEMENTATION
The event catalog is small and can repeat. Different stages adapt context and
speech, not scientifically fitted developmental dynamics. Numerical load, energy,
tension, motives and learning coefficients remain shared experimental assumptions;
they are not child norms or validated age-specific predictions. Internal action
IDs such as work and sketch are legacy categories for task engagement and leisure;
their recorded outcomes use the selected patient's activities. Reviewer traces
are model calculations, not direct knowledge of a child's mental state.
