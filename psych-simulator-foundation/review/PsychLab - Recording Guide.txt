RECORDING APPROACH

1. Prepare and capture the real interview before recording the voiceover. Use
   Jordan, 28 days, seed 42, Standard, with the chosen MDD teaching case for the
   narrated example. This ensures the described hobby and reduced-enjoyment facts
   belong to the selected record. Do not imply the chosen case is a mystery case.
2. The suggested questions are prompts for a recording, not guaranteed outputs.
   Model wording varies. Match the narration to what the actual replies establish.
   Do not insert invented patient speech or substitute mock replies for live output.
3. Use a few readable excerpts from one complete session. If a request fails,
   preserve its context and avoid implying that every live request always succeeds.
   Keep the spoken limitations. The video is a demonstration, not an accuracy study.
4. Zoom into relevant text. Use setup footage briefly, then spend most screen time
   on the interview, evidence note and final debrief. Keep the source excerpt used
   to justify a conclusion visible long enough to read. Avoid scrolling long logs.
5. It is reasonable to edit waiting time for a short video; label shortened waits
   and do not imply real-time response speed. Record a continuous session first
   so edits preserve the conversation order and context.
6. Do a timed read. The narration is 479 words. At 135 words/minute it takes
   about 213 seconds before visual pauses. Aim for 3:40–3:55 total.
   NIMH can be spoken letter by letter; WHO can be expanded to World Health
   Organization if preferred. Small pronunciation differences alter the word count.
7. Use on-screen labels such as Experimental educational prototype and Software
   tests, not clinical validation. Show research publishers as references, without
   endorsement language or institutional badges that imply partnership.
8. Keep credentials, account information and personal access configuration off-screen.
   Finish with actual repository and demo links only when they are verified.

OPTIONAL JUDGE QUESTIONS (NOT PART OF THE TIMED SCRIPT)

What makes this distinct from ordinary patient roleplay?
The patient has a server-held, frozen reference history; language generation is
restricted to selected evidence; and the learner links reasoning back to disclosed
answers. The design supports inspection, while remaining vulnerable to model errors.

Does the personality system recreate human psychology?
No. It separates constructs and implements small, explicit experimental rules for
relationships and goals. Traits are not fully measured, and the dynamics are not
empirically calibrated. The separate population sampler is not used to claim
validated patient personalities.

Does the app grade whether a diagnosis is correct?
It compares selected labels with authored teaching premises and checks evidence
references, missing areas and note structure. It does not establish clinical
correctness or provide validated competence scores.

What would you improve next?
Review a focused set of cases with a qualified clinician or educator, observe
students using the complete workflow, and resolve the documented reliability
failures before making broader educational claims.

What did AI do in the project?
Featherless models supply the live dialogue roles. Local code owns simulation,
state, checks and debrief logic. Coding-assistant support and any offline data
work should be disclosed separately in the submission according to actual use.
